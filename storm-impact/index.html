Storm Impact: river → Strontia Springs Reservoir → Foothills plant

Sensor readings lined up along the flow path, so staff can judge when a storm's impact reaches the plant. The page shows the evidence; the operator makes the timing call.

Provisional data. USGS and Denver Water readings here are provisional and subject to revision. Two storms from provisional data show a pattern, not a proven rule. Denver Water's data terms apply (bottom of page).

1. Flow-path timeline

One row per stop, in flow order, on one time axis. Drag the slider to move the cursor; the orange dashed line marks the river peak, and the blue dotted line marks 4 hours later, when that water reaches the intake (Denver Water's travel time, confirmed by Jake).

2. Reservoir by depth (sonde casts)

Time across, depth down. Each column is one sonde cast (about every 6 hours); blank columns are gaps between casts or short casts that sampled only the top.

Depth is the sonde's "Vertical Position", in metres below the surface (the file gives no unit; Jake confirmed metres). The sonde hangs in the middle of the reservoir, roughly 400 m from the intake, which is nearer the dam (Jake). The time zone of the sonde's clock is not stated; times are shown as recorded.

3. Past-storm comparison

Computed from the files in this repository by build_data.py. Baselines are medians of the days (or casts) before the river peak day.

4. Sensors along the path

storm baselinestorm peak

Marker colour and size show each sensor's reading at the cursor against its own scale for this storm (listed on each card). A dark ring means that stop's peak has passed. Hollow markers have no water-quality data. Rivers: USGS NLDI flowlines. Intake-to-plant line is schematic; Conduit 26's route is not in the repo.

At the cursor

What this does not do: automated prediction, generalized storm modelling, causal proof, validated travel times, or treatment recommendations. Plant timing is only accurate to the day because TOC is a daily lab sample. Water travels gage to intake in about 4 hours (Denver Water raw water group, per guide.md; Jake confirmed this is the travel time to use); the water-quality impact shown here arrives later.

Sources: USGS 06707525 and 06701900 15-minute series (strontia-brief/series/), USGS daily summaries (data/USGS_South_Platte.csv), Denver Water reservoir sonde (data/Strontia 0407_0819.xlsx), Denver Water Foothills influent (data/FoothillsInfluent.csv), coordinates from strontia-brief/places.json.
